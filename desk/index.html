Private · investment agent

Desk watches one investment book all day and shows its work.

Desk is a small, private investment desk that runs as a service on a Mac. It reads a real Interactive Brokers book through Canary, keeps its schedule, memory and every decision in durable storage through Torok, and talks to its owner in plain language. It proposes. It never places an order on its own.

What Desk does

Most of the day it does nothing, on purpose. Work happens when the calendar says so, when the book changes in a way the rules care about, or when the owner asks.

Reviews on the market's clock

A weekday review at 07:00 Berlin. Then preparation an hour before each enabled venue opens, an opening check, a pre-close review with a decision cutoff, and a handover after the close. Canary supplies the session times, early closes included.

Evidence before opinion

Account, positions, risk, regime, calendar, proposals and reconciliation are retained on their own clocks, independent of any model. A decision names its sources, a reason, what would prove it wrong and when it expires.

Ask Desk

A durable conversation with the book. Each question runs interpret, evidence, analyst, review as one recoverable workflow. A separate reviewer model checks the answer against the exact evidence it was given. When something is missing, Desk says so and withholds the recommendation.

Trading stays a human act

Optional, and off by default. A single-leg DAY limit order is drafted by Canary, previewed by the broker, then confirmed by the owner with a passkey. The model cannot reach that handler and a chat message cannot authorise it.

Spending with a ceiling

Every provider call reserves against a monthly allowance before it is sent. Scheduled reviews get a little grace under an absolute cap. Interrupted calls are estimated and reported.

Boring to operate

One binary as a per-user LaunchAgent, health endpoints, upgrades that wait for running work to finish, and macOS notifications for the handful of things that need a person. Idle time costs no model calls.

How it is put together

Desk is mostly configuration. Canary owns everything about the broker: the TWS session, prices, risk, calendars, protection proposals and reconciliation. Torok owns the work: the inbox, schedules, bounded episodes, memory and recovery. HyperServe hosts the console and its live feed on loopback. Desk supplies the instructions, chooses nineteen read-only Canary tools, wires the observers and decides what a decision must contain. Models come from OpenAI's Responses API, one for analysis and another for review and research.

Around the desk

Five views share one masthead, one quote ribbon and the Ask Desk panel. Each figure on screen carries its source and its age, because a number without a clock is a guess.

What it is not

  • Not a product. There is no download and no repository link. It runs one book, mine.
  • No claim of edge. Operational correctness and investment results are measured separately, and building the thing establishes neither.
  • One machine. A user LaunchAgent runs while the Mac is awake and logged in. That is a known limit.
  • Simulation in the pictures. The screenshots use Desk's simulation mode with a synthetic book. The real screens look the same with real numbers, which is exactly why they are not here.

Talk to me

Desk is not published, but the ideas in it are worth arguing about: evidence contracts, a reviewer model that is not the analyst checking its own work, spending that is reserved before the request goes out. If you are building something in this space, I would like to hear about it.
